About Glyph

Glyph is an independent index of literary events in the New York metro area, and a small press for the writers in it.

Two things happen here. The calendar collects readings, open mics, workshops, book launches and panels from the rooms that host them, so that knowing what is on does not require already being inside the scene. And a handful of local poets and authors get their work published in full, on paper-coloured pages, with every right still theirs.

How the calendar is built

A listings agent runs twice a day. For each venue with a verified machine-readable calendar, it reads that calendar, normalises what it finds into a single schema, throws out anything it cannot vouch for, and publishes the result as one file the page reads. Hand-entered listings are merged on every run and always beat a scraped one, because a person who checked outranks a robot that parsed.

It fetches politely: one identifiable request at a time, one second apart per host, robots.txt honoured. These are forty-five-seat rooms, and the cost of indexing them should land on Glyph rather than on them.

Three rules govern the agent, in this order:

  1. Never blank the board. A run that produces nothing writes nothing and raises an alarm. A venue whose feed breaks keeps its previous listings instead of vanishing. The trade is that a stale row is possible — which is why every row links out, and the venue’s page always wins.
  2. Never clobber human input. Corrections made by hand survive every subsequent run.
  3. Never commit noise. An unchanged board is not republished, so the history of the file is a history of the scene rather than of the cron schedule.

What Glyph actually covers

Glyph claims the full metro area — the five boroughs, near New Jersey, Westchester, Long Island. Claiming a region is not the same as covering it, and thin coverage nobody admits to is worse than a smaller promise kept. So here is the real state of it, straight from the generated board: how many venues are in the registry per region, how many of those have a verified calendar the agent can read, and how many listings are live right now.

A zero in the middle column is not a venue that refused; it is a venue whose calendar is not machine-readable yet, and a job still to do. Where a region shows no venues at all, Glyph is claiming ground it has not covered — the table is the apology.

Affiliation, and getting it wrong

Glyph is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or speaking for any venue, bookshop, series, library or institution listed here. Venue names and addresses appear because they are facts about real places that host real readings. Listings are drawn from those venues’ own public calendars and link back to them.

If you run one of these rooms and want your calendar out of the index, say so and it comes out — no argument, no form. If a listing is wrong, that is Glyph’s fault rather than yours, and telling us gets it fixed by hand within a day or so.

Featured writers

Work published here appears under a plain-language grant the writer agrees to when they submit: non-exclusive, revocable, credited, and never licensed onward or fed to a model. The full terms are on the submit page, in language short enough to actually read.

Colophon

Built as a static site: no tracking, no analytics, no cookies, no embedded third-party anything. The only network requests this page makes are for its own stylesheet, its own script, and its own data files. Nothing you do here is recorded, because there is nowhere to record it to.

The marks beside the wordmark are reference marks — pilcrow, dagger, double dagger, section. They are the marks a text carries in order to point at itself, which is about as far as this site goes towards explaining its own name.
