Why a data site for poems

A reading is a piece of data with a terrible half-life. It exists as an Instagram story, a line on a chalkboard, a Squarespace page nobody updated, and then it is Thursday and you missed it. Multiply that by a few hundred rooms across five boroughs and two states and the scene becomes something you can only know by already being in it.

So: writing is data. Not as a gimmick — there is no scansion engine here, no word-frequency dashboard telling you what a poem means. The metaphor earns its keep as a discipline. If the calendar is a dataset, then it has a schema, it gets validated, it cites its sources, it says what it does not know, and it never quietly goes wrong on someone who was counting on it to plan a Thursday night.

Sourced, not scraped blind

Every listing comes from a venue’s own published calendar and links straight back to it. The venue is the authority; Meter is an index.

Coverage is published

Meter claims the whole metro area, which is not the same as covering it. The coverage table prints the gap rather than leaving you to infer it from an empty filter.

A poem is not a row

The calendar gets the data room. Poems and prose get paper, a serif, and a reading measure. Two rooms, on purpose.

Featured writers

Meter publishes whole pieces, not teasers — a few poems or a prose excerpt, on their own page, with the writer keeping every right they started with. Submissions are open and read by a person.

The roster Submission terms

Meter is an independent index of literary events in the New York metro area. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or speaking for any venue, bookshop, series or institution it lists. Times, prices and whether a thing is still happening are the venue’s to state — always check the link.
