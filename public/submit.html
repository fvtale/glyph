Submit your work

Meter publishes whole pieces by poets and authors in the New York metro area. Submissions are open, read by a person, and cost nothing to make.

What to send

Up to five poems, or one prose piece of up to about two thousand words — whichever you would want a stranger to read first. Paste it into the email body if you can; a .docx, .txt or .pdf attachment is fine too. Line breaks matter here and are preserved exactly as you send them, so send it laid out the way you want it set.

Alongside the work:

  • Your name as you want it printed.
  • Two or three sentences of bio, in your own words rather than ours.
  • Where in the metro area you are based — neighbourhood or town, your call.
  • Any links you want on the page: your site, wherever your books are sold.
  • Whether any of it has appeared elsewhere. Previously published work is welcome; we just want to print the credit correctly.

The terms, in full

Sending work means agreeing to these. They are deliberately short, and they are the whole agreement — there is nothing else to sign.

  1. You keep the copyright. All of it. Publishing here changes nothing about who owns the work.
  2. You grant Meter the right to display it on this site, with your name and any credit line you ask for. That is the entire grant.
  3. The grant is non-exclusive. Publish the same piece anywhere else, before or after, without asking us. Simultaneous submissions are fine.
  4. It is revocable, no reason needed. Email and it comes down at the next deploy — usually the same day. You will not be asked to justify it.
  5. No money moves in either direction. Meter does not pay for work and never charges a reading fee. If that changes, it changes for new submissions, not retroactively for yours.
  6. Nothing is licensed onward. Your work is not syndicated, sold, anthologised, or handed to anyone who asks — they get pointed at you. It is not used to train a machine-learning model, by us or by anyone we would give it to.
  7. Edits need your say-so. Obvious typos get fixed. Anything else — a title, a line break, a stanza gap — is asked about first and dropped if you say no.
  8. This is plain language, not a lawyer’s contract. It is written to be read rather than to be airtight. If a clause does not work for you, say which one and we will talk about it instead of you walking away.

How to send it

One email, no form, no account, no third-party submission portal taking a cut of your attention:

Email your submission

contact@datarail.org · subject line: Meter submission

A person reads it. Expect a reply within a few weeks; if a month goes by, send a nudge — that is not rudeness, it is a lost email. A no is a no about fit, on one site, on one day, and nothing more than that.

Listings, corrections and venues

The calendar is swept from venue calendars by a robot, and robots get things wrong. Hand corrections outrank anything automated and survive every later run, so telling us fixes it permanently.

  • A wrong or cancelled listing? Send the link. It is corrected by hand, usually within a day.
  • Running a series we have missed? Send where you post your dates. A page with a real calendar feed gets swept automatically; anything else gets entered by hand.
  • Run a venue and want out of the index? Say so and your calendar is dropped. No argument, no form, no follow-up email asking if you are sure.
